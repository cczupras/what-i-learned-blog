## Productivity hack 📱
### Look up from that display.
This week I have a productivity hack for you. Turn off all notifications - I personally keep my mobile in quiet mode the whole time. Only important messages go to my watch notify me. With this I can focus on the things around me, like nature and people. Every now and then I take a couple minutes to look at the phone or email inbox to work on getting rid of the things that popped up. Either I sort them to read at a later point (create notes in my notes inbox) or take the time to answer (emails and personal messages). Just image there is a person you could meet and when this person passes you, you are looking at your phone instead of spending a smile. Or you miss the rare animal you where looking for during your hike. Once I have read a quote (can’t remember by whom) that if people talk to you, you get a notification on your phone (or laptop) and you look away because you get distracted - it gives the person in front of you the intention that your time is more valuable than theirs. 
Nobody likes to feel not valuable - so look up from that display..

If you have been following my blog you know that a big amount of the text is not directly written but audio recorded. With the help of a pipeline the audio is transcripted, summarized and send to my notes inbox if my second brain (notion).

# #3 Sports, Food & Health 🏋️🏃🚴🥦 {color="orange_bg"}
## I started Hybrid Training
What is this? If we ask [Le Chat - Mistral AI](https://chat.mistral.ai/chat) - here is a definition:
> Hybrid training is a fitness approach that combines different types of exercises, such as strength training and endurance activities, to create a well-rounded and comprehensive workout regimen.
I stumbled upon this term again after reading an article in the training magazin and after talking with a fitness buddy after he succeeded in reaching a fitness milestone of completing a 10k under 40 minutes. I was curious how he trained and he gave me some tips and influencer names. Guess what I did… right put some sources into NotebookLM and created an audio description to listen to during a dog walk. After hearing it and watching some videos I figured something out…
Essentially, it's what I've been doing all along, focusing on strength development and endurance through high-intensity CrossFit training. This approach has always been at the core of my fitness regimen, emphasizing the importance of building strength and stamina simultaneously. I aim to broaden this approach, especially since I've been involved with HYROX over the past two years, which emphasizes running and endurance. This involvement has allowed me to explore new dimensions of fitness, pushing my limits and discovering new potentials within myself.
Over the last year, I've also taken up cycling, enjoying longer rides on my gravel bike. This newfound passion has not only added variety to my training routine but has also provided a refreshing way to experience the outdoors. Cycling has become a significant part of my life, complementing my existing training methods and contributing to my overall fitness journey. It's amazing how incorporating different activities can enhance one's physical capabilities and mental well-being.
As I continue to evolve in my fitness journey, I am excited about the possibilities that lie ahead. By integrating various forms of exercise, I am confident that I can achieve a well-rounded and balanced approach to health and fitness. This holistic perspective not only keeps my workouts interesting but also ensures that I am constantly challenging myself and striving for improvement.
The last years I have not spend much attention to specific training plans or specific goals. I just went to classes and hit whatever the WOD (workout of the day) was. Occasionally I went running or did some lifting in my garage gym.
This will change as I want to experience how training evolves when working to specific goals with a plan. Starting with more attention to the data I am tracking about myself I will also plan the training sessions accordingly.
Hybrid Training I figured out is closest to the method how I would like to train. So here is the plan:
### Weekly Training Plan 📈 {toggle="true"}
	**Training Days (4-5 days)**
	1. **Monday: Strength Training**
		- Focus: Deadlift and Squats
		- Goal: Build strength and increase weight
		- Additional: 5 sets of push-ups with 2-minute breaks
			- Use breaks for mobility exercises focusing on hips and shoulders
	2. **Tuesday: Running**
		- Longer running session or interval training
		- Optional: Replace with bike sprints, especially uphill
	3. **Wednesday: HYROX Training**
		- Attend a HYROX class at CrossFit Flensburg
		- High-intensity training to prepare for HYROX
	4. **Thursday: Active Rest or Mobility Session**
		- Bike ride at a moderate, easy pace (Level 2 or lower)
		- Focus on mobility exercises, especially for hips and shoulders
	5. **Friday: Biking**
		- Biking session depending on time and training fatigue from the week
		- Additional: 5 sets of push-ups with 2-minute breaks
			- Use breaks for mobility exercises focusing on hips and shoulders
	6. **Saturday: Partner Workout (WOD)**
		- High-intensity interval training with a partner
		- Include a variety of exercises for a comprehensive workout
	7. **Sunday: Rest Day**
		- Completely passive or active rest, depending on how I feel
### ➡️ Key Points & Goals:
- **Push-ups Goal**: Aim to do 100 push-ups unbroken.
- **Squat Goal: **5 x 100kg back squat
- **Running Goal: **Half marathon in under 2:00 hours
- **Mobility**: Incorporate more mobility sessions throughout the week to improve flexibility and prevent injuries.
- **Hybrid Training Focus**: Emphasize strength, high-intensity interval training, running, and mobility.

## Umami smoked tofu gyros 🔥
This week I will share a simple but tasty recipe for tofu. You basically only need a block of firm smoked tofu and an asparagus peeler.
Take a bowl, peel slice by slice of the tofu. 
Season it with soy sauce, garlic, onions and your favorite herbs. Optionally add some Sriracha sauce for extra spice. 
Stir fry in a medium hot pan with some oil until crispy. 
Enjoy in a fresh bread with cucumber, Tzatziki and tomatoes or just eat it with rice or french fries.

<summary>**Google’s Prompt Engineering Guide - a summary**</summary>
	🤖 Here is a concise summary of the main points about Prompt Engineering:
	- **Prompt Engineering** is the process of designing **high-quality prompts** that guide Large Language Models (LLMs) to produce accurate outputs.
	- It is an **iterative process**, and **anyone can write a prompt** – you don't need to be a data scientist or machine learning engineer.
	- Effective prompt engineering involves **understanding how LLMs work** (as prediction engines) and **controlling their output** through both prompt design and adjusting LLM configuration settings.
	- Key LLM output configuration settings include **Output Length**, **Temperature** (controlling randomness), **Top-K and Top-P** (restricting token selection based on probability). These settings collectively impact the creativity and relevance of the output.
	- The **Prompt Engineering Guide** details various **prompting techniques** to improve LLM results, including:
		- **Zero-shot prompting** (providing no examples).
		- **One-shot & Few-shot prompting** (providing one or multiple examples, respectively, to guide the model on desired output structure or pattern).
		- **System, Contextual, and Role prompting** (setting overall context, providing specific task details, or assigning a specific identity/persona to the model).
		- **Step-back prompting** (prompting the model to consider a general question before the specific task).
		- **Chain of Thought (CoT)** (generating intermediate reasoning steps to improve accuracy, especially for tasks requiring reasoning). CoT is often combined with few-shot prompting for complex tasks. A temperature of 0 is recommended for CoT.
		- **Self-consistency** (generating diverse reasoning paths and choosing the most common answer, often using a high temperature).
		- **Tree of Thoughts (ToT)** (generalizing CoT by exploring multiple reasoning paths simultaneously).
		- **ReAct** (combining reasoning with external actions/tools like search or code interpreters).
		- **Automatic Prompt Engineering** (using an LLM to generate and evaluate prompts).
		- **Code prompting** (using LLMs to write, explain, translate, debug, and review code).
		- **Multimodal prompting** (using multiple input formats beyond just text).
	- The **Prompt Engineering Guide** highlights several **Best Practices** for prompt engineering. The **most important** best practice is to **provide examples** (one-shot or few-shot) within the prompt, as it serves as a powerful teaching tool.
	- Other best practices include designing prompts with **simplicity**, being **specific about the desired output**, favoring **Instructions over Constraints**, controlling the max token length, using **variables**, experimenting with input formats, writing styles, and output formats like **JSON** to enforce structure and limit hallucinations.
	- For few-shot prompting in classification tasks, it's advised to **mix up the order of possible response classes** in examples to avoid overfitting. Starting with 6 few-shot examples is a good rule of thumb for classification.
	- It is crucial to **adapt to model updates** and, importantly, to **document the various prompt attempts** in detail to track progress and learn.
