The word "Prompt" has several meanings, depending on the context in which it is used. Here are some of the most common meanings:

  1. Prompt or Command Prompt: In computing, "prompt" often refers to an invitation for a user to enter input. This can be in a command line or in a text field.
  2. Quickness: In general language use, "prompt" can also mean "quick" or "immediate," as in "giving a prompt response."
  3. Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning: In this context, "prompt" refers to the input or text given to a model to generate a specific output.

#2 Tech

Prompting 📣

Recently I started not just chatting with different AI’s but also to refine some prompts and also save these in my second brain as reference. This way I am able to reuse them for certain tasks I need to do more often. One example is the new section song of the week - where I want to have the same structure every week. The basics are searched by the AI and I only add a personal note and change some wording.
I have watched a webinar about prompting which was quite inspiring so I share it with you:

Resource Link index.html

In my company I have just joined a working group for AI. This week one of our topics was how we can use prompting to achieve better results. We had a really good discussion and some examples. I also got to know there is a challenge for the prompt of the week - so one that other colleagues could use to help with something in their daily business. I took some great ideas out of the meeting. One I want to share is that you can also use AI to create and refine a prompt with you. Nice idea I think.
Just tell you favorite AI chat briefly what you want to achieve and ask it to build a prompt for this. Of course this is just a starter but may be it helps starting in that topic.
Another inspiration I got from a podcast (german):

Resource Link index.html

Basically the idea here was to ask the AI to challenge you - ask you questions and check if you have understood the answer fully that you asked in the beginning.

"You are here to question and challenge me. No matter what we discuss, phrase your responses in a way that encourages me to think. Come up with meaningful questions whose answers will help me better understand my task or goal. Then, review my answers with me until I am satisfied with the solution.”

Give it a try - may be share your experience with me - I am always looking for useful prompts.

This week I also got a Copilot license to explore how we can use it in our company. You can already use the free version in MS Teams. The subscription version also allows you to use predefined prompts and also to create Agents. Guess what - yes I started to build my first agents - at least I tried. I do not quite understand yet how they are working or which benefit they bring in comparison to a saved prompt but I think I will get there.
Well of course I will not share details here but one thing which was helpful and saves a lot of time was to build an agent that operates on our App changelogs. For example I took the changelog and asked the Agent to create me a brief description and some bullet points about the main functionality of the App. That of course needs still some refinements but now I am able to use this agents basically on all our App changelogs and create a nice description and key feature list in no time. And the benefit - they all have the same structure and tonality.

Read my workout analysis and personal review …

I'm quite happy with my performance, although I'm not excelling at it. But that was known. My fitness is not there where it should be, or where it could be actually.
So, yeah, I'm on fire to train again a bit more properly. A bit more strength training, that is definitely needed. It probably also helps with biking for the next season.
So yesterday, 25.3:
The workout was really nice. It was a lot of fun. I didn't make it to the end. I lost my strength in the end, and the snatches felt quite heavy.
The wall walks had quite an impact as they fatigued my shoulders. I actually implemented some change in my wall walks. Normally I keep my breath when I walk up the wall. This time I focused on breathing, so with every hand move I took a breath. And that was a game changer - at least for getting more air in. It was really good. I didn't feel that I'm running out of air during the wall walks. Afterwards, in the lift, I had to gasp for air a bit more than I anticipated. The weights got heavy for me.
But yeah, that's how it is. So, all in all, the Open was really nice workouts. It was more or less classical CrossFit.
No fancy movements. All movements were known and you do them in training. Maybe I should do wall walks more often. Helps to bulletproof the shoulders, better overhead coordination and breathing technique. I would definitely give them a shot a bit more.
I had a good time in the Open this year. Looking forward for next year when I'm a bit more prepared, hopefully.
But first, bike season and also preparing for the next HYROX in November. Still a lot of time, but I think I need to level up my running game…

About the Artist:
Fall Out Boy is an American rock band from Wilmette, Illinois. Known for their catchy melodies and profound lyrics, Fall Out Boy has built a loyal fanbase over the years. The band, consisting of Patrick Stump, Pete Wentz, Joe Trohman, and Andy Hurley, has had numerous hits and released several successful albums.
Background of the Song:
"The Phoenix" was released in 2013 and is part of the album "Save Rock and Roll". The song is about resilience and new beginnings. Inspired by the mythical figure of the phoenix rising from the ashes, it symbolizes the ability to rise again after setbacks.
