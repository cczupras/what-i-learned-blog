#2 Tech

Best friends: Excel and ChatGPT

Excel and ChatGPT make great partners. This week, I came across a YouTube video packed with tips and tricks about ChatGPT, one of which really stood out to me: using images, like screenshots, to describe or support your questions.
I immediately put it to the test…

Read more…

… with a recipe card for a delicious dinner, where I wanted to extract the ingredients and instructions to store them as text in my recipe database. And to my surprise, it worked perfectly. Now, I’m brainstorming the next step—automating the process. The idea is to take a photo of a recipe, store it in a dedicated online folder, and trigger a pipeline that, with the help of ChatGPT, extracts the recipe and ingredients, sending it straight to my Notion recipe database. I’ll keep you posted on how this evolves.
Another use case where I’ve found the image upload feature in ChatGPT helpful is with Excel. To be honest, I’ve never quite excelled at logging my work hours. In the past, I had to reconstruct a workweek by cross-referencing chat conversations, version control protocols, emails, and my calendar. It’s time-consuming and not particularly enjoyable. Since one of my goals this year is to be more productive, I decided I needed a simple offline solution to log my time and perform basic calculations. Additionally, I sometimes need to add context and descriptions to the tasks I’ve worked on, as project managers occasionally request more details. So, I decided to use Excel for this.
I wanted a main sheet that aggregates yearly totals, a sheet to store basic data (like customer projects, cost centers, and project task numbers), and a daily log sheet where I could track my time. Naturally, I wanted a template for the daily sheet that I could copy each day and just fill in the date. Setting up the basic data was simple—just create a table with the necessary information. But then I hit a roadblock: How could I select that data in the daily sheets? And could I set up a dropdown where I could first choose a project, and then automatically populate the related project codes in another cell?
I built the table structure, took a screenshot, and asked ChatGPT for help. It walked me through the process in detail and even offered to create an example Excel file for download. I didn’t realize ChatGPT could generate output files—that was a pleasant surprise. From this, I learned how to name tables, use the Name Manager, and set up field and data validation for dropdown lists.
Now, my Excel sheet is always open while I work, making it easy to quickly log my tasks and the corresponding time. At the end of the day, I have an aggregated time total for each project code that I can then transfer to our time logging system. My next idea? Automating an export from the daily data that I can simply copy and paste into the time logging system to make the transfer even smoother.
Give image usage with ChatGPT a try and let me know what cool things you learned…

#3 Sports, Food & Health

Crispy fried rice balls

As most of the days are packed and time is valuable I always try to improve some recipes from our cooking box in the matter of making it easier or being able to get it done faster. In this recipe for a tofu curry there are rice balls included that needed to be stir fried. I changed that step.

Read more …

Instead of frying the rice balls in a pan with oil which also makes a lot to clean I thought I could put the balls into the air fryer. So I cooked the rice, put some curry spice on it after it was cooked and cooled down with cool water and added some flour. Next was taking some rice into my hands and mould little balls. I put those into a glas bowl and brushed a little bit of oil onto them.
10 minutes into the air fryer on 200°C and they were crispy and ready to eat without a lot of mess. I will definitely try this again with different spices, may be herbs, different oil (sesame ?), may be also mix some nuts in as well for flavour.
What can I say, I have added this new skill to my cooking repertoire and they go really nice with a curry to add an extra crunch.

#4 Misc

Email Inbox and MS Todo

Productivity first in 2025.
First day of work started a bit different this year. Instead of digging into one email after another I took a different approach.

Read more…

I opened MS Todo next to my inbox and added tasks for items that needed follow-up, instead of reacting to each email immediately. This way, I read through all emails first and then took action where needed. Some issues had already resolved themselves or were handled by colleagues, requiring no further action from me. Getting through my inbox felt quicker, and now I have a prioritized task list for scheduling and planning my work. It was quite satisfying to start the first working day feeling relaxed and productive. An added benefit was having time to chat with colleagues between reading emails—since I wasn't immediately diving into detailed analysis tasks or implementing fixes.
Since this first day I have now used MS Todo everyday to add plan task for the day. It also shows me suggestions of marked emails for follow up. Very nice so I can mark my todo emails now and sort them into the right folder but still see there is a todo. When there is a reminder set then it automatically is added to my day in MS Todo in the morning.
Some little nice automation that keeps me focussed on the important things each day.
Are you using it too? What is your experience? Do you have another useful tip for me?
