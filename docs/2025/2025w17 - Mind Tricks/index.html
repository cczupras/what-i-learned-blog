#1 Personal ✒️

Personal Note

Let me start with a quote this week:

"People are all born with a love of learning, but the fixed mindset can undo it. Think of a time you were enjoying something—doing a crossword puzzle, playing a sport, learning a new dance. Then it became hard and you wanted out. Maybe you suddenly felt tired, dizzy, bored, or hungry. Next time this happens, don’t fool yourself. It’s the fixed mindset. Put yourself in a growth mindset. Picture your brain forming new connections as you meet the challenge and learn. Keep on going."

– Mindset - Updated Edition: Changing The Way You think To Fulfil Your Potential von Carol S. Dweck
As you noticed I have picked up the book again and read a bit further. If you interested in Mindset and how the mind can shift or be changed I highly recommend reading the book.
But back to the quote - it highlights for me what this blog is about. I believe I have - most of the time - a growth mindset. I always want to learn more and more - may be sometimes too much, then I have to decide and prioritize.
Since starting this blog I have established quite some new habits - learning a foreign language every day, writing each week with reviewing my notes, pushups. But also I have changed my priorities in life a bit. I take more time to meet friends and people I relate with. Regular training sessions in the sports I love. This feels quite fulfilling - it is the small decisions and small changes that make an impact.

AI is still a hot topic and will be throughout the rest of the year and it will definitely dominate the topics in this blog. This week I again learned a lot about different areas around AI. I do not focus on the technology as such but also on the topics around it - the humans that build and use AI.
I think in todays world it is even more important to consume information from a broad base of publishers and various source to not get biased in your thoughts and opinions. Most of the time there is so much more if you view things from different angles.

Enjoy reading and don’t forget to leave some feedback.

#3 Sports, Food & Health 🏋️🏃🚴🥦

Burning \~4900 calories in one day



Yesterday I thought it would be a good idea to take the bike and drive to the Team WOD Class in our CrossFit Box.
Then I thought, riding back the same route is quite boring, I could take a different route back home, maybe along the coast to see some water - I like riding the bike along the coast and have a look at the sea, it gives a feeling of freedom to me.
Also it shows how beautiful our nature can be.
So the first part was quite good the bike just rolled effortlessly - I almost made it under one hour.

The Team WOD was a really good one - a good mix of movements, luckily no bike erg involved. Me and my team partner Noah had a really good flow and splittet the work in small parts where we could move through the workout without longer breaks. Thanks for the workout it was quite fun.
After the WOD and some minutes of rest and a lot of fluid to refuel I felt quite energized so I took on the planned route along the coast line.
Some kilometers further I noticed I would need a bit of calories - the snacks I had after before and after WOD were not enough. Luckily there was a McDonalds on the way and I got 2 hot apple puffs. One I enjoyed right away and the second was a bonus when reaching the coast for a small break.
The last 15k were quite a challenge. I could feel the drain of energy but this was the challenge I wanted to tackle - see if I feel comfortable to go more kilometers on bike.
Finally arriving home was a satisfying feeling - a lot of effort and the longest time in the saddle this year. 100k on bike is not so far away. Then after a short break I ate quite a lot of food to re-fuel.
Key learnings for me here:

  • I had packed enough fluid and drank in good intervals. (pro tip: I added sugar beet syrup to the water. It provides a lot of carbs, is cheap and also has some minerals)
  • Breaks were good, also from a timing perspective. I is good to plan a stop (even short ones) every 20 to 30k.
  • I need to pack more snacks: oat bars, banana, dates
  • Big portion of oatmeal / porridge in the morning (1h before the tour start) was sufficient.

Profiles, System or Master Prompt

However I do not want you to leave with out gaining at least some knowledge.
This week I have attended a webinar and the speakers showed something very simple but astonishing powerful. The have called it the “Master Prompt Method”. Basically if your boil it down it is making use of System Prompts or Profiles.
Some Chat Bots allow to add data to your account which should be considered when chatting and prompting. For example claude.ai is making use of Profiles within their personalization of the account. I really like using claude.ai - it produces high quality output, has a good writing style and to my opinion is quite clean. Also I like the user interface.
Ok, let me explain what the magic is here. You know that the LLM needs data to predict the next words and it needs context to put the right weights in relation to the input. So imagine you can add a lot of information about yourself, your business into the model so that it knows you and your preferences - it would then give you better answers tailored to your understanding of the world and your natural language.
Normally you would need to add all this to a prompt when asking more complex questions. But you do not want to save the data somewhere and copy it into every prompt, this would be cumbersome. You would rather want to use this information implicitly.
Now there is different ways to approach this. With claude.ai you have the possibility to add this data (a massive amount of data) in the personalization of your profile:

Resource Link index.html

Other providers might offer a similar approach - I have not tried others than claude yet. Another idea could be to create an agent which you can create - for example with Microsofts Copilot - and add the data to the system prompt.
I am starting to explore this area a bit more - playing around with different profile information and system prompts in agents. There will be more content related to this in the next weeks.
